DFT Simulations of Moiré Superlattices

Research by Cameron Getner

Using Density Functional Theory (DFT), we simulate the behavior of electrons in moiré superlattices—materials created by stacking and twisting two-dimensional layers at specific angles. These systems exhibit exotic quantum behaviors that challenge our understanding of condensed matter physics.

Our current investigation focuses on two competing quantum phases: the trimer phase and the honeycomb phase. We hypothesize that an emergent Dirac cone in the electronic band structure may be the key mechanism driving the transition between these phases.

Trimer Phase

Fidget Spinner Pattern

The trimer phase exhibits a three-fold symmetric pattern that resembles a fidget spinner. In this configuration, electrons organize into triangular clusters, creating a distinctive rotational symmetry.

Physical significance: This phase may emerge at specific filling factors and represents a strongly correlated electron state with broken rotational symmetry.

Honeycomb Phase

Hexagonal Arrangement

The honeycomb phase features a hexagonal lattice structure where electrons arrange themselves in a pattern reminiscent of a honeycomb or graphene's crystal structure. This phase exhibits six-fold symmetry.

Physical significance: The honeycomb arrangement is energetically favorable under certain conditions and may support topological electronic states.

Band Structure Analysis

Emergent Dirac Cone

The electronic band structure reveals the energy-momentum relationship of electrons in the material. The most striking feature is the emergent Dirac cone—visible where the red line intersects the conical structure.

At the Dirac point, the conduction and valence bands touch, creating a unique electronic state where electrons behave as massless particles, similar to photons. This gives rise to unusual transport properties and quantum Hall effects.

What is a Dirac Cone?

A Dirac cone appears when two energy bands meet at a point with linear dispersion. Near this point, electrons obey the Dirac equation (relativistic quantum mechanics) rather than the standard Schrödinger equation, leading to exotic quantum phenomena.

Key Findings

Two Competing Quantum Phases

We have identified two distinct electronic ordering patterns in our simulations: the trimer phase (resembling a fidget spinner pattern) and the honeycomb phase (hexagonal arrangement). These phases represent different ways electrons self-organize in the moiré superlattice.

Emergent Dirac Cone

The band structure reveals an emergent Dirac cone—a conical intersection in the energy dispersion where electrons behave as massless relativistic particles. This feature is visible where the red line intersects the cone-like structure in our band structure plots.

Phase Transition Mechanism

We suspect that the emergent Dirac cone plays a crucial role in mediating the transition between the trimer and honeycomb phases. This hypothesis connects topological features of the band structure to real-space electronic ordering—a fundamental question in quantum materials research.
